## 1. What we need from you

**Booking request: `POST /bookings`** (our report, sections 4.1.1 and 4.1.4)

Events books a Venue for an Event by calling us directly, not through the Gateway, with the header `X-Service-Name: events`.
Body: `eventId`, `promoterId`, `venueId`, `firstDay`, `lastDay`, with dates such as `2027-06-08` and both days included, and optionally `replacesBookingId` (a Rebooking, below).

`promoterId` must be the Promoter's user id: the id the Gateway has for the Promoter, which it sends as `X-User-Id`. Section 3 explains why.

We answer:

- `201` with the Booking: what you sent, plus its `id` and `amount` (EUR as a decimal string, such as `"800.00"`).
- `409` when the Booking can't be made: `/problems/days-taken`, `/problems/event-already-booked` or `/problems/venue-unlisted`. These are a normal outcome to show the Promoter, not errors to retry.
- `422` with `/problems/unknown-venue` when no Venue has that `venueId`.
- `400` when the body is malformed, `lastDay` is before `firstDay`, or `firstDay` has passed.

We don't check the Venue's capacity, and our answer doesn't carry the Venue's name, city or capacity: read them with `GET /venues/{venueId}` (section 2) before you send the Booking request.

**Rebooking: `POST /bookings` with `replacesBookingId`**

This is our answer to your postponement and change of place (section 3, point 2). Send a Booking request as above, with `replacesBookingId` set to the Event's current Booking. In one transaction we cancel that Booking and create the new one, so either both happen or nothing changes:

- If the new Booking can't be made (`days-taken`, `venue-unlisted`, `unknown-venue` or `400`), the old Booking stands, as your `postponeEvent` and `updateEvent` require.
- The old Booking's days don't count against the new ones, so postponing by one day at the same Venue works.
- `422` with `/problems/unknown-booking` when the Booking you name doesn't exist or belongs to another Event. That is also the answer when the Venue Manager cancelled it just before; you'll get the `BookingCancelled` for it.
- `409` with `/problems/booking-ended` when the Booking you name has ended.
- The new Booking gets a new `id`, and its `amount` is worked out at today's price per day.
- Once we answer `201`, the old Booking is gone and can't come back. Make your own checks on the Venue before the request, and if saving your Event fails afterwards, save it again rather than cancel the new Booking, which would leave the Event with no Venue.

Use it whenever an Event that already has a Booking needs other days or another Venue: `postponeEvent`, `updateEvent` with a new place, and `bookPlace` on a draft that already has a place. A Booking request without `replacesBookingId` for an Event that has a Booking stays `409 /problems/event-already-booked`.

**Booking cancellation: `DELETE /bookings/{bookingId}`**

Optionally with `?reason=...`, which we pass on in `BookingCancelled`. This is what your `deleteEvent` needs. We answer `204` only once the days are free. `404` if no Booking has that id, which is also the answer when the Booking is already gone: cancelled by the Venue Manager, or through Event cancelled. `409` with `/problems/booking-ended` if the Booking has ended.

**Event cancelled** (our report, section 4.2.1)

Your `EventCancelled` (your section 4.2) is what we need. When it arrives, we cancel the Event's Booking, so you don't need to cancel it yourselves too, as your `cancelEvent` already assumes.
Fields we use: `type` from your common header, and `eventId`, the same id you sent in the Booking request. We don't need `reservationId`. We ignore every other message on your exchange.

**Conventions** (our report, section 4.1.3)

UUIDs for ids, ISO 8601 dates, whole days in the Europe/Lisbon time zone, amounts in EUR as decimal strings with two decimals, and errors as RFC 9457 `problem+json`. G3 has adopted them.

## 2. What we send you

**`BookingCancelled`** on our exchange `places`, routing key `BookingCancelled` (our report, section 4.2.1)

We publish it for every cancellation, whoever made it, including the old Booking of a Rebooking. You can ignore those with `cancelledBy: events`, which you caused yourselves. When the Venue Manager cancels, it is the only way you learn that your Event has lost its Venue (section 3, point 7).
Fields: `type`, `bookingId`, `eventId`, `promoterId`, `venueId`, `cancelledBy` (`events` or `venue-manager`), and `reason`, left out when none was given.

Please key what you store on `bookingId`, not `eventId` (our report, section 4.2.3). Delivery is at-least-once, so a late copy of an old cancellation could otherwise seem to cancel a newer Booking for the same Event, and after a Rebooking an Event has had more than one Booking.

You don't need `BookingConfirmed`: our answer to your Booking request already tells you the Booking exists.

**Venues: `GET /venues/{venueId}` and `GET /venues`** (our report, section 4.1.1)

Call both directly with `X-Service-Name: events`, as you call `POST /bookings`. `GET /venues/{venueId}` returns a Venue, Listed or Unlisted, with its name, address (including the city), capacity and Rating. `GET /venues` searches the Listed Venues by a date window and a number of days, a minimum capacity, a maximum price per day, a city, a minimum Rating and a Venue type, and returns each Venue with its Rating. For `requestPlaces`, the window is the Event's first to last day, and the number of days is the number of days in it.

## 3. Where your report and ours differ

1. **`promoterId`** (your 1.1 FR1, 1.3, 2 and 4.3). Your Promoter has an id of its own besides the user id, and your Event stores that id. We ask you to key the Promoter by the user id instead, so that `promoterId` is the user id everywhere. A separate id is only worth having if a Promoter could be shared by several users, such as a company several people act for, or if one user could hold several Promoter profiles. Your FR1 rules out both: "Each user corresponds to at most one promoter." What the second id costs:
   - You: Event Management resolves the user id to a `promoterId` on every command (the first row of your Collaborations table), which your 3.2 point 8 accepts as a cost.
   - Everyone else: Billing (G3) compares `promoterId` with `X-User-Id` to check who pays a bill, and Reviews (G1) to check who may rate a Venue. With two ids, each would need your mapping. We pass `promoterId` on unchanged in our messages, and we can't translate it, because you call us directly, so we never see the Promoter's `X-User-Id`.

   Keyed by the user id, the Promoter keeps its tax ID and contact email, the Promoter Service still says whether a user is a Promoter by whether that Promoter exists, and the lookup goes away. Your rule that commands take the user from the Gateway, never from the request, stays as it is.
2. **Moving a reservation** (your 2.3, Main flows and 4.4). You assume we can move a reservation to new dates. We don't: a Booking's days never change once it is made, because the days and the amount have already gone to Billing and Reviews in `BookingConfirmed`. The Rebooking in section 1 gives you what your flows need instead: nothing changes if the new Booking fails. It also covers the change of place, where your flow reserves the new place before releasing the old one. With us that second Booking for the same Event would be refused, since an Event holds at most one Booking.
3. **Whole days, not time slots** (your 4.4). A Booking takes a Venue for whole days, priced per day, as the professor told us to consider it. Send as `firstDay` and `lastDay` the dates, in Europe/Lisbon, of your Event's `startDateTime` and `endDateTime`: an Event from 20:00 to 02:00 the next night books two days.
4. **Names in what you send us** (your 1.3, 2.3 and 4.2). On our API and in our messages, a reservation is a Booking with a `bookingId`, and a place is a Venue with a `venueId`. Your report's `reservationId` is our `bookingId`, and its `placeId` is our `venueId`. What you call them inside your service is up to you.
5. **No `PlaceUpdated` in delivery 1** (your 3.2 point 6 and 4.4). We won't publish a message when a Venue changes, for now. Your 3.2 point 6 already reads the Venue with `GET /venues/{venueId}` when you book, when you publish and when the owner reads the Event, and that keeps your copy close enough. Until the next read, your public catalogue may show an old name or city, and a capacity cut below `maxTickets` goes unnoticed, which is no worse than today: we don't check Bookings against capacity either. Adding the message now would mean a new message, schema and requirement on both sides two days before the delivery, to fix data that is only a little stale. We may add it in a later delivery.
6. **`/venues` is ours** (your 3.2 point 6 and the notes to 4.1). The Venue is `GET /venues/{venueId}`, not `GET /places/{id}`. Your `/venues/{placeId}/rating` and `/venues/{placeId}/review-eligibility` would clash with our paths, since we've asked G4 to send every path under `/venues` to us. Could you move them under a prefix of your own?
7. **When the Venue Manager cancels** (your 1.2 and 4.4). A Venue Manager can cancel a Booking for an unforeseen reason, and the professor asked for this. Your report doesn't consume `BookingCancelled`, so your Event would stay PUBLISHED with a Booking that no longer exists, and your Event states have none for a published Event without a Venue. What happens to the Event then is your decision.
8. **Ratings** (your 2.2 `requestPlaces`). An option, not a request: our search already returns each Venue's Rating, which we keep from Reviews' messages, so `requestPlaces` doesn't need a call to G1 for each Venue. The reviews themselves are still G1's.
