# Open points

Delivery 1 leaves some points open, and this section collects them: what Billing's choice on refunds means for Promoters, what delivery 1 leaves out, and the questions for the professor and the other groups. Wiegers and Beatty advise marking each piece of information that is still missing, rather than leaving the gap unstated, and resolving it before the requirements it affects are implemented [@wiegers2013, ch. 10]^["Sometimes you know that you lack a piece of information about a specific requirement. Use the notation TBD (to be determined) to flag these knowledge gaps. Plan to resolve all TBDs before implementing a set of requirements." (p. 188)]. For each question, this section gives the position the report takes until the answer comes.

## Paid bills are not refunded

Billing (G3) creates the bill from *Booking confirmed*, and cancels it on *Booking cancelled* if the Promoter hasn't paid it yet. A paid bill is not refunded, whoever cancelled the Booking; G3 has told us this is deliberate for delivery 1. It matters most when a Venue Manager cancels after the Promoter has paid: the Promoter has lost both the Venue and the amount. Places can't change this on its own, because bills belong to Billing (Table \ref{tab:ownership}). A Rebooking is different: Billing carries the old Booking's paid bill over to the new Booking, so a Promoter who postpones doesn't pay twice.

## Out of scope for delivery 1

Delivery 1 leaves out Venue approval, transferring a Venue to another Venue Manager, hourly or seasonal pricing, distance search, keyword and equipment filters, blocking days outside Bookings, refunds and cancellation fees, paying the Venue Manager a Booking's amount, and *Venue updated*, a message telling other groups that a Venue's details changed. Events reads a Venue with `GET /venues/{venueId}` when it needs it, so *Venue updated* is a candidate for a later delivery. The Scope in the Application requirements section says where each stands. One of them is open rather than settled: blocking days waits on question L1 below. Refunds are Billing's to decide, and the section above gives its choice.

| L10 | G2 | Will Events call Places directly, not through the Gateway, with `X-Service-Name: events`, and adopt the conventions of Table \ref{tab:conventions}? | Answered: yes. Places' `409` and `422` answers reach the Promoter as business errors. |
| L11 | G4 | Will the Gateway send the user's id in `X-User-Id` or in a signed token, and is that id a UUID that never changes and is never reused? | We assume the header and a permanent UUID (Table \ref{tab:identity}), following the convention of UUIDs for ids, since Places stores the id as each Venue's Venue Manager (FR-5) and Billing compares `promoterId` with it. | L13 | G1 | What does `GET /api/v1/ratings/venues/{venueId}` answer for a Venue with no reviews? May Places call it directly, not through the Gateway, with `X-Service-Name: places`? G2 drops `/venues/{id}/rating`, and serves review-eligibility at `/events/review-eligibility` unless Reviews agrees to drop it, so every path under `/venues` is Places'. |
| L15 | G2 | Will Events consume *Booking cancelled*, so that it learns when a Venue Manager cancels its Event's Booking? | Answered: yes. Events consumes *Booking cancelled*, matches it to its Event by `bookingId`, and ignores it when `cancelledBy` is `events`. When a Venue Manager cancels, a draft Event loses its Venue and stays a draft, and an Event that Events has made public is cancelled, so G2 publishes *Event cancelled* and ticket holders are refunded. |
